Direct D -> P,  first set only
Only aligned n16 -1.32%(p=.014) and n512 -1.36%(p=.016) are significant. Median paired changes are smaller, -.60% and-.78%. All other direct rows are not significant. Unaligned n16 is +.49%, unaligned n512+.10%;unaligned n8192+.86%, aligned n8192+.59%. No equivalence claim follows from those nonsignificant results. The direct P experiment therefore has two isolated roughly 1% gains, not a useful broad or repeatable benefit yet. Normal-Wago short losses remain despite fewer tail branches.

The proposed stopping rule is reasonable
Repeat normal->DR's full 25 rows; repeat the fixed 22 directD/P rows; repeat the same 22 normal/P rows; repeatD/DR memory's four timers. This retains aligned/unaligned short, parity, cache, streaming and controls. Original n63 adverse evidence remains even though it is outside the reduced repeat. State which original rows are unrepeated; do not label the entire 44-row matrix confirmed. If a later promotion claim depends on excluded boundary rows or compile cost, repeat them before that claim.
Freeze the confirmation count list and a practical-benefit criterion before sampling. A repeated lone 1% gain is not automatically sufficient to justify 25 extra native bytes and retained normal-Wago short losses. The user's 5% target is an initial guide, not an absolute gate; judge effect size, confidence, relevant coverage and costs together. No PR timing is required if P does not show a useful incremental benefit. Correctness wiring can remain as an opt-in reproducibility case without a combined performance claim. If DR confirms its allocation/time benefit independently and P fails, the evidence can support keeping DR and rejecting P/PR performance promotion. Do not force the combination because it already exists.

Initial conclusion
P has not proved a useful mitigation of D's short-loop costs. The bounded repeats are appropriate to check the two small direct gains and guard against an early rejection. DR's count mitigation has a clear byte cost and remains a separate experiment. Promotion remains unsupported before complete repeats, controls, validation and report review.
