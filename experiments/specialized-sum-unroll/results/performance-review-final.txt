Limits that must stay in the report and recommendation:
1. One CPU, with turbo enabled, powersave governor, active desktop, and sibling CPU 3 not isolated. Interleaving and separate repeats reduce drift risk but do not prove a portable architecture result.
2. Execution fixtures use zero extra live parameters. High-pressure cases prove correctness, selection, and fallback, not high-pressure throughput.
3. Repeated calls use the same input buffer. Larger than L3 covers streaming traffic, but no hardware-counter measurement proves the exact cause of losses/gains.
4. Scalar first iteration and 3/7/15-element remainders are measured. Gains are total-function gains, not isolated branch or dependency measurements.
5. Eight-chain setup shifts group address from 0xbf to 0xca. The evidence cannot attribute changes solely to independent accumulators. Whole-candidate profit comparisons remain valid.
6. Default corpus controls verify the normal compiler with experiments disabled. They do not show real corpus speedups or adjacent corpus behavior with D enabled. The report already states this.
7. C is rejected after a large, consistent first-run loss; it does not require repeat for promotion because it is not proposed. A/B/D/E gains have separate repeated process samples. D is the strongest balanced candidate, not uniformly fastest for every count/alignment.
8. Nonsignificant compiler timing changes do not prove zero added compile cost. Unchanged measured allocations are a stronger, directly observed result.
