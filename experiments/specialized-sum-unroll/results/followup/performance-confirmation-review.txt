Compilation, allocation and native-cost limits
Every new raw CompileModule comparison has equal bytes and allocation counts: pressure0 26072 B/29 allocs; pressure12 26296 B/30 allocs. Every new compile time difference is not significant. Threshold compile estimates range approximately -4.30% to +2.79%; their p-values do not support a faster/slower compiler claim. H repeat pressure0 -5.24% has p=.383; it is not a credible compile speedup. No added backend compilation cost has been measured reliably, so a backend break-even count based on a positive median alone must be labeled a noisy estimate.
Earlier corpus evidence remains valid: public CompileFull/memory D adds 1792 B and 2 allocations, from the two slice-growth sites. Raw-backend no-allocation results cannot be extended to the public API. Final H/T64 public corpus costs still need review.
Earlier native inspection remains applicable: common adapter44; kernel baseline total347, D410, H467, T64 462, T128/T256465 bytes; pressure0 frame56 and operand spill counters0. H adds120 bytes vs baseline and57 vs D. T64 adds115 vs baseline and52 vs D. These are diagnostic fixture sizes, not every real function. Operand spill counters exclude local/frame/ABI traffic. H four-tail dispatch and changed group offset are costs. T128/T256 group offset differs by3 bytes because the gate immediate is wider. Do not attribute every gain solely to branch reduction.

Methodology limits and required report corrections
1. Retain every adverse and non-significant row, including H n16 repeat and direct H unaligned n8192. Do not imply H/T64 are globally faster or that streaming gains repeat.
2. Define reported percent changes as ratios of medians. Median paired ratios differ in noisy rows. Benchstat p-values use the supplied distributions; the sampling order is paired/interleaved, but those tests are not paired statistical tests. p=0.000 should be written p<.001.
3. Multiple rows/designs were examined. A lone p=.017/.024 result is exploratory evidence, not a family-wise error-controlled result. Repeated large cache effects provide stronger evidence. Do not base promotion on the geomean of a chosen synthetic count set.
4. One CPU, one host, boost enabled and the stated shared-desktop environment remain limitations. Cache-resident and streaming results do not establish another architecture's result.
5. FOLLOWUP-PLAN.md:24-25 still says the linkname bridge targets a private 'record'. It now calls a private scalar-argument setter. Correct this stale description.
6. Need final corpus confirmation/control and final follow-up report before recommendation sign-off. No new real-application eligible loop has been shown; the synthetic memory.sum result must be labeled as such. Keep initial linked_list public compile loss and use its same-tagged-binary control, not an assertion that layout explains it without evidence.

Current conclusion
Evidence supports further study of D, H and T64 for selected cache-resident sums. H improves D's small-count behavior but pays a larger code cost and loses to D in the direct unaligned64KiB row. T64 retains repeat cache gains and does not show significant short-count losses in this confirmation; its confirmed streaming gain remains below5%. No compile allocation increase appears at the raw backend level, but the public API has a verified D allocation increase. No new production default or promotion recommendation is justified until corpus evidence is complete, and absent real-application admission remains a limit. No blocker to keeping the opt-in experiment and evidence in a draft PR if these qualifications appear in the report.
