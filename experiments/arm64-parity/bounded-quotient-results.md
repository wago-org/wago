# Exact bounded signed quotient (Apple M4 Max, arm64)

Isolated baseline c74d21c1; regional-memory-read is off. Experimental catalog option `bounded-signed-quotient` defaults off. Environment enable for a default build: `WAGO_ARM64_EXPERIMENT_BOUNDED_SIGNED_QUOTIENT=1`. The compiler matches only bytecode semantics; it never reads corpus names, manifest arguments or expected results.

A bounded private-reader scan recognizes i32.div_s followed by a same-local quotient tee, signed maximum with lo, signed minimum with hi, and the final same-local set/tee. Bounds satisfy lo<=0<=hi, lo<hi, -lo<=4095 and hi-lo<=4095. The final assignment is included because otherwise the intermediate local would still hold max(q,lo), potentially observed later. Older aliases of the destination are realized before any overwrite; all source registers are read-only, protected through allocation, and W result writes zero-extend.

For d>0, the exact test `uint64(int64(n)-int64(lo)*int64(d)) < uint64(int64(hi-lo)*int64(d))` identifies a quotient in range. Both products and the offset fit signed i64 for the admitted i32 operands and constants. Inside the interval, ordinary signed division already yields the clamped result. Below it, the exact clamped result is lo; at/above the upper threshold it is hi. Outside the interval, the sign of n distinguishes the bounds because lo<=0<=hi. Boundary quotients equal to a bound are also correct when the conservative test chooses saturation. No floating-point conversion or reciprocal approximation is involved.

For d<=0, the original zero and MIN/-1 overflow checks precede ordinary signed division and both clamps. An earlier pending trap is materialized before the new packet. Normal setLocal handles the final version and its semantic facts; a distinct pinned destination can be written directly after older aliases have been preserved. The scan is fixed-length and does not perform global control-flow analysis.
